            how to copy

            Getting a FEN or a PGN

            A FEN is one position. A PGN is a whole game. Often you need neither: copy the page’s link and paste that.

            A Chess.com puzzle

            Daily puzzle, today or any day: nothing to copy. Use Today’s or Pick a date by the board, or paste its link, like chess.com/daily/2026-10-28.

            Rated Puzzles or Puzzle Rush: these need your Chess.com login, which this page can’t see (your browser keeps each site’s sign-in to itself). When the puzzle ends, open it on Chess.com’s analysis board, then:

            1. Tap Share under the moves.
            2. Open the PGN tab. The FEN sits just above the PGN.
            3. Copy the FEN and paste it here.

            Your own game on Chess.com

            Quickest: copy the game’s address, chess.com/game/live/…, and paste it.

            Website:

            1. Open the game from your Game History.
            2. Tap Share at the bottom of the panel next to the board.
            3. Open the PGN tab. Copy the PGN for the whole game, or the FEN above it for the position on the board.

            Android app: open the game, tap More at the bottom left, then Export PGN.

            iPhone or iPad app: open the game and tap Share. It gives a link; paste that.

            Someone else’s game

            Type their username in My games on this page. Every public game shows up; tap one to replay it.

            Or on Chess.com: open their profile, then Games, open the game, and follow the same Share steps.

            On Lichess

            Open the game and scroll below the board to Share & export (on the analysis page it’s the FEN & PGN tab). Copy the FEN or the PGN.

            Or just copy the address. This page reads Lichess game, puzzle and study links.

            Then paste it in the box. A FEN gets Put it on the board; a PGN or a link opens with Open on Chess.com.

            Go over your own games

            Type a Chess.com username and each game shows up as its last position. Tap one to replay it on the analysis board, with Game Review if you’re signed in. Anyone’s public games work.

              Before your first puzzle

              Once on each device is enough.

              Open a blank Chess.com board and switch its Analysis toggle off, so the engine can’t show you the answer. Chess.com remembers it on that device. Switch it back on when you want to check your idea.

              Chess.com always opens a position with White at the bottom. When it’s Black to move, use its flip button first.

              Put this page on your home screen

              iPad, in Safari: tap Share (the square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen, then Add.

              Android, in Chrome: tap the ⋮ menu at the top right, then Add to Home screen, then Add.

              The icon opens this page in your normal browser, where you’re already signed in to Chess.com.

              When the daily puzzle changes

              Chess.com changes its daily puzzle at midnight US Pacific time. In India that’s 12:30 PM from March to early November, and 1:30 PM from early November to March, when the US clocks go back.

              So in the morning you still see the puzzle dated yesterday. That’s Chess.com’s today, not a stale page.

              The puzzle card shows when the next one comes out, in your own time. Leave this page open and it switches to the new puzzle by itself.

              Why not my rated puzzles?

              Chess.com’s rated Puzzles, Puzzle Rush and Puzzle Battle live behind your Chess.com login, and Chess.com gives outside sites no way in. Only the daily puzzle is public.

              This page can’t borrow your login either. Your browser keeps each site’s sign-in locked to that site, so askthemove.online can never see your Chess.com cookies. That lock is what keeps every account on the web safe.

              So for rated puzzles, solve them on Chess.com. If you want one on this board, copy its FEN there (see Where do I find a FEN or PGN? by the paste box) and paste it here.

              How Stockfish works here

              Stockfish is the strongest chess engine there is. This page uses its small “lite” version, a 1.8 MB download, and runs it on your own phone, tablet or computer. Nothing is sent anywhere.

              It downloads once, in the background, while you can already move the pieces. Your browser keeps it for 30 days, so it starts straight away next time.

              It never shows a score or a move by itself. Tap Check my move after you play one and it says how good it was. Only Show Stockfish’s move gives the answer away.

              With Data Saver on, it waits until you tap Check my move.

              Change engine under the board lists the other engines. On a computer you can swap in the full Stockfish 19, the strongest one here, a 99 MB download. Big engines show a warning first and ask twice, and you can stop the download. If an engine can’t start, the page goes back to Lite by itself.

              A bookmark that opens today’s puzzle

              A backup for when this page isn’t handy. Run it while you’re on any Chess.com page.

              iPad: bookmark any page and name it Puzzle. In Bookmarks tap Edit, tap Puzzle, replace its address with the code and tap Done. To use it, open a Chess.com page and tap Puzzle in your bookmarks.

              Android: bookmark any page with the ☆, tap Edit, name it Puzzle and replace its URL with the code. To use it, open a Chess.com page, type Puzzle in the address bar and tap the bookmark that shows up.
