The problem

The engine says Nf5. You still don’t know why.

A game review gives you a score, a best move and a line of moves six deep. All of it is correct, and all of it is written for an engine.

What you wanted to know is smaller and harder. What did I miss? What should I look at next time? What happens if I try my own idea?

Without those answers, the same mistake turns up again next week, in a different game.

AskTheMove is built to answer three questions:

  • ?

    What did I miss?

    The threat, the loose piece or the square you didn’t check, pointed out on the board.

  • ?

    Why is the engine’s move better?

    The reason in a few sentences, taken from lines that were actually calculated.

  • ?

    What if I play my move instead?

    Play it out as a branch, see the strongest reply, then come back to the game.

How it works

Evidence first. Then the explanation. Then your own question.

The panels below are drawn for this page, using the trap from the top of the page. The positions, lines and engine figures in them are real.

  1. 1.

    Evidence

    Stockfish searches the position and a rules library replays every line. Material, checks and threats are settled as facts before anything is said about them, and each score shows how deep it was searched.

    Works today

  2. 2.

    Explanation

    The tutor turns that evidence into a few plain sentences, tied to arrows on the board. It is given only checked moves and facts, so it explains what happened instead of guessing.

    In development

  3. 3.

    Branch

    Ask “what if?” and play it. Your line is saved as a branch of the game, a branch can have branches of its own, and each note stays on the position where you wrote it.

    Works today

  4. 4.

    Return

    Go back to the actual game whenever you like. The game report keeps your key moves in order, so you can move on to the next moment that mattered.

    Works today

Who it’s for

Adults rated about 800 to 1600 who already review their games.

It’s probably for you if

  • You open the engine after a loss and the best move still doesn’t make sense.
  • You would rather understand one mistake properly than skim through ten.
  • You play online or over the board and train on your own time.

It isn’t built for

  • Help during a live game. It analyses finished games, positions you set up, and practice games against its own computer opponents.
  • Memorising opening theory. Other tools do that well.

How much does it cost?

When does the beta start?

Invites go out in small groups once the tutor is ready for real games. There’s no fixed date yet. The waitlist is how you’ll hear first.

What devices does it work on?

It runs in a web browser on a desktop, laptop or phone. There’s nothing to install.

Where do the explanations come from?

Stockfish, a free and open-source chess engine, searches the position. A chess rules library checks legality, material and every line that is shown. Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, then writes the explanation from that evidence. The tutor is built to explain only what the evidence supports.

Can I use it with games from other sites?

Yes. Paste the PGN of any finished game. Most chess sites can export one.

Can I use it during a game?

No. It analyses finished games, positions you set up, and practice games against its own computer opponents. It won’t help you in a live game against another person.

What happens to my email?

It’s stored in a Cloudflare D1 database and used only to send beta news. It isn’t sold or shared. Ask for it to be deleted at any time. The privacy notice has the details.
