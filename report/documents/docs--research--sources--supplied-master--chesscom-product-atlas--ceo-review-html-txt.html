<section class="hero"><div class="eyebrow">CEO review</div><h1>The moat is not “online chess.” It is the closed learning loop.</h1><p>Chess.com combines liquidity, identity, content, analysis, training, community and events. A parity build that copies navigation but omits the loop will look familiar and still feel empty.</p></section>
<section class="grid grid-2">
<div class="card"><h3>What matters most</h3><ol><li>Fast route to a satisfying game.</li><li>Immediate post-game explanation.</li><li>Personalized next practice.</li><li>Visible progress and identity.</li><li>Friends, clubs, events and streaks that create return pressure.</li></ol></div>
<div class="card"><h3>What not to fund first</h3><p>Do not begin with every variant, every club-admin feature, broadcast production or a course marketplace. Those are breadth engines, not the minimum learning loop.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Commercial logic</h3><p>Keep core chess generous. Monetize high-frequency improvement, deep analysis, content breadth, convenience and family bundling. Exact prices are regional and must be re-observed before business modeling.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Strategic risk</h3><p>Matchmaking liquidity, fair play, content quality and explanation trust are coupled. A weak result in any one can poison the whole product.</p></div>
</section>
<section class="section"><h2>Recommended clean-room sequence</h2><div class="card"><ol><li><strong>Stage 1:</strong> account, legal bot play, saved games, puzzles, basic lessons and deterministic review.</li><li><strong>Stage 2:</strong> human Live/Daily infrastructure, ratings, reconnects and safety.</li><li><strong>Stage 3:</strong> personalized training, Insights, social graph and clubs.</li><li><strong>Stage 4:</strong> tournaments, events, broadcasts, creator content and commerce expansion.</li></ol></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="callout warn"><strong>CEO verdict:</strong> The opportunity is credible only if the product is meaningfully better at helping a player understand one confusing move. “Chess.com, but smaller” is not a strategy.</div></section>
